# Audit of 2026-11-07

Three independent read-only reviewers, run on the user's request: security, process and cost (from
`sf metrics` and the job records of the last 90 days), and code. This file keeps their joint
conclusion and what came of it. Ticket keys of other workspaces are replaced by placeholders.

## Conclusion

The core works well, but the boundary around the agent was more porous than assumed, and the factory
had worked almost only on itself.

### 1. Security: fix before more product work

The smoke of that morning proved that the sandbox kept the agent away from the secret **files**. The
audit found other ways out (credible, not tried live):

- **Critical: Unix sockets.** The sandbox allowed connecting to every Unix socket. Through the session
  D-Bus a command could ask systemd to start a program **outside** the sandbox. Docker and the
  Tailscale daemon were reachable too.
- Git **hooks** ran without a sandbox at the factory's push, in the user's checkout.
- The agent's **file tools** (Write, Edit) were outside the sandbox; only the permission classifier
  kept them away from the user's checkout.
- **WebFetch** was an open channel out of the Implementer.
- The **Codex gate** could read everything (for example `auth.env`), and its findings were not redacted.
- The **dashboard token** was written to the system journal, and over Tailscale anyone with the link
  could act.

### 2. Direction

- 91% of the jobs and 90% of the cost went to the factory itself; none of the 7 product jobs was
  confirmed merged in the product repository.
- Measure outcomes, not only cost: how much the user changes handed-over work, whether the MR merges,
  how fast, and follow-up bugs.

### 3. Task files (the coordinator's part)

Task files had grown to 1,600–1,800 words for 200–300-line changes, close to pseudo-code, with 2–3
plan-check rounds whose cost was not counted.

### 4. Reviews

- The Codex gate earns its cost: 94 blocker or major findings, 58 fixed. In 72 of 155 iterations it
  found a blocker or major item after a clean Claude review.
- The Claude review found 20 such items (6 fixed) for 18% of the cost.
- Jobs with 4 or more iterations were 19% of the jobs and 36% of the cost.

### 5. Code

- Tests did not gate the merge; the coordinator ran them after merging.
- The stop rules were copied in three places (server, explanations, dashboard), and one had drifted.
- `static/app.js` was 8,400 lines without types; comments read as a changelog.
